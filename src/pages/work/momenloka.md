## An invitation with a job to do

A digital invite should feel personal. It also has to tell guests where and when, and help the host keep track of who’s coming. MomenLoka does that with an invitation builder, templates and RSVP, plus a photobooth service for weddings and events.

It has three parts, each with its own job. The website explains the offer. The editor is where someone actually makes their invite. The visual identity keeps the whole thing looking like one product.

## Making room for the invitation

Work on the invitation editor included a layout rework. An editor has to give space to both the content being made and the controls used to change it. The invitation itself is the product, so the surrounding interface needs to support that task without overwhelming it.

Templates and invitation covers provide a starting point. The broader asset work includes faceless illustrations, logo variants, posters and cover templates. Those materials give the product a visual vocabulary beyond a single landing page.

## Product and presentation together

The landing page copy and pricing presentation explain the service before a visitor enters the builder. Blog and analytics work extend that public experience, while the invitation editor and RSVP address the practical work of preparing an event.

MomenLoka uses Next.js and Convex with shadcn/ui. The scope crosses application development and product presentation: layout, reusable visual assets, content and the service’s public entry point.

## Keeping the distinction clear

An invitation builder and a photobooth service support the same occasion, but they are different offers. Clear presentation matters so visitors understand what they can create online and what belongs to the event service.

The editor, preview and public invitation share a structured configuration renderer. That gives the same invitation data a consistent route through editing and presentation. Convex holds the application data for invitations, templates, RSVP and orders.

## What this work demonstrates

MomenLoka is an example of building software with an expressive output. The technical interface and the visual result have to work together, from the first template choice to the invitation a guest opens.
